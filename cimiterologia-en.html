Cimiterologia

Expanding life by crossing through Death

Scroll

In the shadow of the Pyramid of Cestius, where cats doze on the graves of Gramsci and Keats, where Shelley and Camilleri rest, Amelia Rosselli and Dario Bellezza, one of philosophy's most unexpected entrances opens: the Non-Catholic Cemetery of Rome, a three-hundred-year-old garden that keeps silence in the heart of Testaccio.

Cimiterologia is a cycle of encounters on death and dying through the conscious crossing of cemeteries. Born on January 12, 2025 from the collaboration between Andrea Colamedici and cemetery director Yvonne A. Mazurek, this journey transforms taboo into philosophical practice: not an architectural study nor morbid curiosity, but a way to expand life by crossing through death, with feet and with words.

The encounters take place in the small chapel built in 1898 by German architect Andreas Holzinger, alternating moments of listening, silence, and dialogue with intimate and conscious walks through the cemetery. Each session is an existential pit-stop where one can pause to reflect on one's own finitude, away from the hysteria of visible cities, facing a mirror that allows no superficiality.

The Non-Catholic Cemetery, declared a Monumental Zone of National Interest in 1918, contains over four thousand burials of people from around the world: English, German, American, Scandinavian, Russian, Greek. Protestants, Orthodox, Muslims, Buddhists. The inscriptions on the gravestones speak in over fifteen different languages, composing a silent chorus of finished lives that continue to question the living.
