**Name:** Andrea Colamedici

**Title/Role:** Philosopher, Publisher, Writer. Co-founder of Tlon (Rome). Author of "Prompt Thinking" (Polity Press, 2026).

**Short Bio:**
Andrea Colamedici is an Italian philosopher who works at the intersection of philosophy and artificial intelligence. He co-founded Tlon, a cultural project in Rome that includes bookstores, a publishing house, and a school of philosophy. He teaches Prompt Thinking at the European Institute of Design and 24Ore Business School in Rome, and "Borges and Artificial Intelligence" at the Universidad del Sur in Buenos Aires. He chairs the Scientific Committee of GenIALab, an AI research hub at AANT in Rome.

**Why this conversation would be compelling:**

In 2025, I published a philosophy book under the name of a fictional Chinese philosopher, Jianwei Xun. The book was called "Ipnocrazia" — it analyzed how digital media manipulates reality. It was written in collaboration with ChatGPT and Claude. The book was reviewed by Le Monde, cited by the New York Times, discussed in Wired and El País. No one questioned whether the author was real. When the truth emerged, it became an international case study — not about AI deception, but about the structures of legitimation in our information systems. The book's thesis — that we live under a form of hypnotic governance — was proven by the book's own reception.

This experiment connects to a larger philosophical framework I develop in my new book, "Prompt Thinking" (Polity Press, February 2026). The core thesis: human thought and large language models share a deep structural similarity. Both operate through patterns, context, and activation. AI is not a replacement for thought — it is a mirror that reveals how thought actually works. I introduce the concept of the "oversubject": a distributed form of cognition that emerges at the interface between human and artificial intelligence.

I also have a book coming out in March 2026 called "Archipelago of Realities" about the fragmentation of shared reality — how the technologies that promised universal connection have produced parallel worlds, each with its own truths, facts, and narratives.
