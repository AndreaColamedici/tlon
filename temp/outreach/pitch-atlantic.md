**Subject:** The Archipelago of Realities: How the Internet Fragmented Truth Into Competing Worlds

---

Dear Ideas Editor,

The internet was supposed to create a universal commons of knowledge. Instead, it has produced what I call an "archipelago of realities" — parallel information ecosystems where different groups inhabit fundamentally different factual worlds. This is an epistemological shift, and the familiar frameworks of misinformation and filter bubbles do not capture what has actually occurred: the infrastructure of legitimation itself — peer review, critical media, institutional authority — has become fragmented. Each island in the archipelago has its own criteria for what counts as credible, its own internal coherence, its own resistance to external verification.

I would like to pitch an essay of approximately 4,000 words that explores this fragmentation through a concrete case study. In January 2024, I published a philosophy book called *Ipnocrazia* under the name of Jianwei Xun, a Chinese philosopher I invented. The book — co-written with ChatGPT and Claude — analyzed how contemporary power operates through the manipulation of perception. It was reviewed by Le Monde, cited by the New York Times, discussed in Wired and El País. No reviewer questioned the author's existence. The operation succeeded because the information landscape was already archipelagic enough to absorb a fiction as fact — each sphere of reception applied its own criteria of credibility, and none of those criteria included verifying whether the author was a real person.

The essay would examine how we arrived at this condition, why artificial intelligence has accelerated the fragmentation rather than resolving it, and what intellectual work is required to navigate political and cultural life in a world of irreducibly plural realities. The argument draws from my forthcoming book *Archipelago of Realities* (UTET, March 2026), and connects to the broader framework of *Prompt Thinking* (Polity Press, February 2026), which examines the structural convergence between human cognition and large language models.

I am an Italian philosopher and co-founder of Tlon, a publishing house and school of philosophy in Rome. I chair the Scientific Committee of GenIALab, an AI research hub in Rome, and teach at the European Institute of Design, the 24Ore Business School, and the Universidad del Sur in Buenos Aires. My work has been covered by Le Monde, the New York Times, Wired, El País, La Tercera, and Corriere della Sera.

I would be grateful to discuss this pitch further.
